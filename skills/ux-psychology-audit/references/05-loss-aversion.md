# 5. Loss Aversion (and status quo bias)

**Idea:** Losing something hurts roughly twice as much as gaining the same thing feels good (Kahneman & Tversky, prospect theory). People also protect what they already have. A "gain" pitch with a free "Maybe later" exit has no psychological weight; a concrete loss does.

## Checklist
- Is the screen framed around gains ("Get more storage") or concrete losses ("These 3 files will be removed on Friday")?
- Is the loss specific and personal (named files, streaks, saved work, expiring credit) rather than abstract?
- Is the loss real and verifiable?
- Does the dismiss option acknowledge the cost ("I'll risk it") without shaming?
- Is there a clear, reversible path to avoid the loss?

## Red flags
- Generic benefits list + "Maybe later" with zero consequences.
- Fabricated deadlines, fake countdowns, or invented scarcity (dark pattern: remove or replace with true information).
- Guilt-trip copy ("No, I don't care about my data") that shames the user. Flag as confirmshaming.
- Loss framing that causes real anxiety around health, money, or safety without justification.

## Recommendation pattern
Replace the benefits pitch with the user's actual at-risk assets, state what happens and when, keep the dismissal neutral but honest, and offer a one-step fix.

## Scoring
0 = pure gain framing, no stakes · 1 = vague urgency · 2 = concrete loss but weak path or tone · 3 = real, specific, honest loss with a clear action; or 3 if the screen intentionally has no stakes and none are needed (explain)
