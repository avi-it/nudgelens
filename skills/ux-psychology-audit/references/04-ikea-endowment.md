# 4. IKEA Effect & Endowment Effect (investment creates ownership)

**Idea:** People value what they built (IKEA effect) or merely feel they own (endowment effect) more than equivalent things made by others. If the user creates or customizes something before the commitment point, leaving feels like abandoning their work.

**Evidence (illustrative):** Duolingo lets you pick a language, set a goal, and finish a lesson before asking for an account, so ~10 minutes are invested before signup.

## Checklist
- Does the user make any meaningful choice or create anything before the signup/payment screen?
- Are choices personal (name, goals, theme, card style, preferences), not just generic?
- Is there something visible that "belongs" to them (a profile, a draft, a workspace, a plan)?
- Does the CTA language continue the experience ("Continue", "Save my plan") rather than interrupt it ("Sign up")?
- Is the effort small and enjoyable, not a chore?

## Red flags
- Signup page with only email + password and nothing owned by the user. Closing the tab costs nothing.
- Personalization asked after signup when it could precede it.
- Heavy effort with no payoff (too much labor reduces value).
- Trapping the user's data to discourage leaving. Distinguish from healthy ownership: users should still be able to export and delete.

## Recommendation pattern
Move customization/first-use ahead of the account wall → show the evolving artifact → rename the CTA to a continuation → keep the effort short (a few quick choices).

## Scoring
0 = nothing owned at the commitment point · 1 = trivial choices · 2 = real choices but after the account wall · 3 = meaningful pre-commitment creation with visible ownership
